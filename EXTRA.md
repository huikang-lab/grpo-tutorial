I tried two short experiments at L512. Each is a single 80-step run with seed 42, so differences of about two points are within seed noise. Each changes one thing about a main run, and every rollout is in the rollout viewer.

Experiment 1: drop negative advantages
[figure extra1.pdf]
I am curious why we even need the failed sequences in training. I thought we could train the model similarly to SFT on correct traces, just that the traces are generated by the model.

Starting from Dr.GRPO L512, every negative advantage is clamped to 0 (starter_posadv.py).

This is what I observed
- Apparently the removed failed sequences do help in improving the performance. The run ended at 50.6%, 8.1 points below Dr.GRPO (58.7%). It also dipped from 48.4% at step 20 to 42.4% at step 30.
- Up to step 10, both runs cut off about as many eval answers (563 and 539 of 1024). After that, Dr.GRPO kept getting shorter (417 at step 80). This run stalled (503). I currently cannot think of an explanation for this pattern.
- When the model did answer, it was still almost always right (99.8% against 99.3%). It just ran out of budget more often.

Experiment 2: a length-penalized reward
[figure extra2.pdf]
starter_lenpen.py changes only the training reward:
- A correct answer scores 1 minus 1e-6 per completion token.
- Anything else scores 0, including a well-formed wrong answer.
- The length penalty is tiny. It is meant to break ties between correct answers.

To allow the length penalty to matter, I needed to reinstate the group normalization removed by Dr.GRPO.

This is what I observed
- The length penalty nudges the model to produce shorter sequences.
- In a group where all eight answers are correct, the rewards differ only by the penalty. Dividing by their tiny std turns this into advantages of order 1 (+0.45 and -2.14 in one group at step 80), which nudges the model toward shorter sequences as strongly as toward correct solutions.
