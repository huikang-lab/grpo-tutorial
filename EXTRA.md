I tried two short experiments at L512. Each is a single 80-step run with seed 42, so differences of about two points are within seed noise. Each changes one thing about a main run, and every rollout is in the rollout viewer.

Experiment 1: drop negative advantages
[figure extra1.pdf]
I am curious why we even need the failed sequences in training. I thought we could train the model similar to SFT on correct traces, just that the traces are generated by the model.
Starting from Dr.GRPO L512, every negative advantage is clamped to 0 (starter_posadv.py). Better-than-average rollouts are reinforced, and nothing is pushed down.
- Apparently the failed sequences do help in improving the performance. The run ended at 50.6%, 8.1 points below Dr.GRPO (58.7%). It also dipped from 48.4% at step 20 to 42.4% at step 30.
- Up to step 10, both runs cut off about as many eval answers (563 and 539 of 1024). After that, Dr.GRPO kept getting shorter (417 at step 80). This run stalled (503). I currently could not think of an explanation for this pattern.
- When the model did answer, it was still almost always right (99.8% against 99.3%). It just ran out of budget more often.

Experiment 2: a length-penalized reward
[figure extra2.pdf]
starter_lenpen.py changes only the training reward:
- A correct answer scores 1 minus 1e-6 per completion token.
- Anything else scores 0, including a well-formed wrong answer.
- The length penalty is tiny. It is meant to break ties between correct answers.
The run uses GRPO's advantages, divided by the group's reward std, with Dr.GRPO's constant-length sum, so per-sequence length normalization does not cancel the length signal.
- The std division makes the tiny penalty a full-size signal. In a group where all eight answers are correct, the rewards differ only by the penalty. Dividing by their tiny std turns this into advantages of order 1 (+0.45 and -2.14 in one group at step 80). That is as large as the gap between right and wrong.
- Correct answers shrank to 143 tokens (221 for GRPO). Accuracy peaked at 60.8% at step 70, then fell to 54.5%. The model started answering before it had found the answer: only 78.8% of its answers were correct, against 99.1% for GRPO.
- So the penalty works, but the std division makes it compete with correctness. It only matters in all-correct groups, where the std is tiny. A floor on the std (say 0.1), or a separate length advantage with a small weight, would keep it a tie-breaker.
